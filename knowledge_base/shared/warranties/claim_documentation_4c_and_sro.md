# Warranty Claim Documentation: 4 C and SRO

## Purpose

Warranty claims must explain what the customer reported, what caused the failure, why the repair is covered, and what the technician did. BMS uses the four C's:

- **Complaint:** the customer's reported problem
- **Cause:** the diagnosed cause of the failure
- **Coverage:** the warranty basis for the repair
- **Correction:** the repair performed

## Documentation Requirements

Before completing a warranty invoice, confirm that:

- All four C sections are complete.
- The technician's report supports the Complaint, Cause, and Correction.
- The failed component and root cause are identified.
- Progressive damage is described when additional components were damaged by the original failure.
- The failure date, Date in Service, hours, or mileage are consistent.
- The claim narrative accurately reflects the work performed.

Warranty text must be written in English according to the training material. Use **Build Cause Narrative** and **Build Correction Narrative** when available, then review and correct the generated text.

## SRO Documentation

When `99-999` is used, the SRO must clearly explain:

- The action performed
- The time associated with the action
- Why an available SRT did not cover the time
- Any relevant travel or additional work

Each repair action should be traceable to the technician's report. A vague or unsupported explanation can cause the warranty claim to be rejected or reduced.

## Progressive Damage

Progressive damage is additional damage caused by the original root failure. Document the causal sequence in the technician's report and the claim narrative. Include every component being claimed and explain how the original failure caused the additional damage.

## Final Review

Review the four C's, SRO, Failure Code, SRTs, labor allocation, parts allocation, and claim type together. The record should tell one consistent story from customer complaint through completed repair.

## Résumé en français

Les quatre C sont **Complaint, Cause, Coverage et Correction**. Ils doivent être remplis avant de fermer une facture de garantie. Les textes de garantie doivent être rédigés en anglais et correspondre au rapport du technicien. Lorsqu’un code `99-999` est utilisé, le SRO doit expliquer clairement l’action et le temps supplémentaire. Les dommages progressifs doivent aussi être décrits et reliés à la cause initiale.
